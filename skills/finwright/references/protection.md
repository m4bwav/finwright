# Protection checklist (optional)

Opt-in. Offer it once a year after a net worth check, or when the user asks about insurance, a will or beneficiaries; skip it if they decline. Ask the questions, note the gaps in plain words, and send specifics to a licensed insurance agent, a fee-only planner or an estate lawyer. This is orientation, not legal or insurance advice. Nothing here is computed or stored by fw.py.

Source: *The Bogleheads' Guide to Investing*, 2nd ed. (2014), ch. 21 and 22. Dollar figures in the book are dated and not used here.

## The rule behind the questions

Insure only losses the user could not pay for themselves, take the largest deductible they can afford, and buy from highly rated insurers. Skip cover for small or narrow risks (extended warranties, flight or accident-only policies).

## Insurance questions

1. **Umbrella liability.** Does the user's liability cover (home, car, umbrella) roughly match what a lawsuit could take from their net worth?
2. **Disability.** Is their earning power insured? Good policies cover their own occupation, start paying after 90 days or less, adjust for living costs, pay partial benefits and run to about 65.
3. **Life.** Does anyone depend on their income? If so, is there term cover sized to the survivors' shortfall after other income? No dependents, or enough assets to be independent, usually means none is needed. Cash-value policies carry high commissions; ask why one was chosen.
4. **Long-term care.** For someone in their late fifties with middle-sized assets: has it been considered? The very wealthy can pay their own way; people with few assets rely on Medicaid.

## Estate questions

5. **Will.** Is there a current will, naming guardians for any minor children? Needed even with a trust.
6. **Beneficiaries.** Does every retirement account, brokerage account and policy name a beneficiary and a contingent one, and do they still match the user's wishes? Bank accounts can use payable-on-death and brokerages transfer-on-death titling.
7. **Trust.** If there is a living trust, have the assets actually been retitled into it?
8. **Power of attorney and healthcare directive.** Has the user named someone to act for them on money and on medical decisions if they cannot?

Report the answers as a short list of open items. The user decides what to do about each.
