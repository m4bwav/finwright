# Self-employed and side income

For a user with income from a side business, a game, plugins, freelancing or a one-person company, when it touches their records or the next-dollar question. This is orientation, not a tax return: finwright does not prepare taxes. Dollar limits change yearly, so check the IRS before quoting one, and send a real decision to a tax professional.

The base is Weltman, *J.K. Lasser's Small Business Taxes 2024* (Wiley), checked on 2026-11-05 against later law. The 2025 tax act (P.L. 119-21) changed several of its rules; those checks rest mostly on professional summaries, not the statute.

## Hobby or business

- Income is reportable either way. A hobby's expenses are not deductible, and the 2025 act made that permanent. The book, written earlier, expected a partial deduction to return after 2025; it did not.
- Profit motive is judged on the facts, and the burden is on the taxpayer: separate books and bank account, a written plan, time spent, changing methods to improve results, know-how, and any profitable years. An activity that is fun gets more scrutiny.
- Profit in 3 of 5 years creates a presumption of profit motive. Form 5213 postpones the question but extends the audit window for those deductions. The author's view is that filing invites attention and rarely helps.

## What to set aside

- Self-employment tax is about 15.3 percent of 92.35 percent of net earnings (the 12.4 percent part stops at the year's wage base). Half is deductible. Net profit, not revenue, is the base.
- The qualified business income deduction is up to 20 percent of that income below the income thresholds. The 2025 act made it permanent. It does not reduce self-employment tax.
- Home office: the space must be used regularly and exclusively for the business, and a shared den fails. The simplified method was $5 a square foot up to 300 square feet in the book; the 2026 rate was not confirmed.

## Retirement plans

- SEP IRA: employer contributions only, with very little paperwork. Limit for 2026: 25 percent of compensation, up to $72,000.
- Solo 401(k): adds an employee deferral ($24,500 for 2026) inside the same overall cap, so at modest profit it allows more than a SEP. It has more paperwork.
- A sole proprietor's "25 percent" works out to 20 percent of net earnings after half of self-employment tax, because the contribution reduces its own base. Do this in fw.py or a checked worksheet, never by hand in the reply.
- A person with a 401(k) at a day job shares one employee deferral limit across plans.

## Rules the book predates

The 2025 act is reported to have brought back immediate deduction of domestic research and software costs (section 174A), 100 percent bonus depreciation, and higher reporting thresholds for Form 1099-K and, from 2026, Forms 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC. Sources are summaries, and one conflicted on bonus depreciation, so confirm with the IRS. Income must be reported whether or not a form arrives.

The book's section on in-house app costs does not mention section 174, and a search of it found no discussion of that section. For someone paying to develop software, do not rely on it there.
